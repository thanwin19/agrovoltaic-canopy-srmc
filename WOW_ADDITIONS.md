# Additions to the one digital twin (November 2026)

Everything below lives inside the single twin, `Review3_Digital_Twin/results/live_twin/SRMC_Digital_Twin.html`, as four new tabs and three new fault options. The code that builds them is in `Review4_Control_and_Simulation/sensor_controller/patch_wow.py` (run after `patch_twin.py`, `patch_mech.py`, `patch_safety_water.py`; safe to re-run). The second copy of the twin that another session had made (`Digital_Twin_Software/`) was moved to `_archive/second_twin_removed/`. Everything is simulation; nothing here uses hardware.

## 1. Ground map tab (Review 2)
3-D ray cast of the 25 m x 25 m array: for every ground point the beam is traced to the sun and the sky is sampled with 96 directions. Day, time of day and tilt are selectable; two maps side by side (controller angle against any fixed tilt); red = heavy shade, yellow = partial, green = well lit.
- Code and data: `Review2_Solar_and_Shadow/code_reference/shade3d.py`, `results/ground_heatmap/` (1,104 maps, 4 days x 25 hours x 12 tilts).
- Check against the 2-D engine in the middle of the array (80 cases): mean difference of the light fraction 0.046. The 3-D result is higher on average (0.32 against 0.28) because shadow can leave through the ends of the rows. In low winter sun the difference is large (up to 0.23), which is the 2-D model ignoring the north-south component of the sun.
- Example, 21 March noon: controller (15° at that hour) 19 % of open-field light on average and 49 % of the land in heavy shade; fixed 45° 47 % and none in heavy shade.

## 2. Structure tab (Review 1 and 4)
- **Three-objective trade-off** (electricity, crop income, structural demand at 140 km/h): `pareto3/`. Structural demand is scaled from the strength table of the final design with two wind-load models (A flat plate, B code-like conservative). Under model B any storm angle from 57° up gives the same governing demand (0.76, the tube) and a flat stow fails (1.38); under model A a flat stow would be best (0.38). The 90° stow is therefore kept, and the question needs a wind-tunnel or CFD run. The controller with the 90° stow has the same demand as a fixed 60° panel with 23 % more electricity (241.6 against 196.5 kWh/m²); fixed tilts of 40° and below exceed the capacity of the final structure.
- **Finite element check of the lever arm** (`Review1_Design_and_CAD/fea/`): plane-stress P2 elements, scikit-fem. Validated on a plate with a hole (stress concentration within 2 % of the textbook value), mesh-converged, and the peak along the arm agrees with the hand calculation (91 against 92 MPa). The arm as first drawn (90 mm root, 48 mm tip, 14 mm thick, SS316 yield 205 MPa) has a factor of safety of 2.3 at the 4.1 kN design load but 0.9 at the 10.2 kN limit of the drive wire, so it was widened to 120/70 x 16 mm (4.7 and 1.9). The 3D mechanism viewer shows the revised arm. Also: the torque tube as a continuous beam gives 10.0 kNm hogging, as in the strength table.

## 3. Design explorer tab (Review 4)
NSGA-III (pymoo) on the twin's own engine: ground coverage, clearance, upper tilt stop and controller weight against solar income, crop income, structural steel and the share of irrigation met from rain. 40 designs for 30 generations (1,187 evaluations): 11 designs on the front. None of the 1,187 evaluated designs beats the final design on all four goals together. The front is an approximation, not a proof. Steel is a screening mass from the column, tube and rope sizes.

## 4. Edge AI tab (Review 4 and 5)
- **Small network for the ESP32** (`esp32_model/`): 10-48-48-1, 2,929 weights (11.7 kB as float32, about 3 kB as int8), trained on 30,000 hours of the reference site to copy the angle search from noisy readings. Single hours: 98.2 % of the best possible (full optimiser 99.3 %, monthly plan 96.8 %, fixed 45° 92.6 %). Closed loop over the 864-case year: **97.3 %**, which is better than a fixed 45° (91.2 %) but **below the monthly plan (98.8 %) and the full controller (99.5 %)**; trained at one site only (97.0 % on average over seven sites). So the network is for boards that cannot run the search. The C code (`srmc_nn.h`) was compiled with the Zig C compiler and gives exactly the same angle as the Python model on 400 test vectors; the Arduino sketch is not flashed or tested on a board.
- **Sensor attack test** (`attack_test.js`): if the anemometer is spoofed to read near zero in a storm, the panels stay unstowed (132 of 132 storm minutes) and the wire force is above the 10 kN winch limit for 40 minutes on average (all of it in the 32 m/s storms). A monitor that compares the anemometer with the wind implied by the tension of the drive wire detects the attack (about minute 241 of the day) and no storm minute is unprotected; a slow ramp-down is caught later (7.5 minutes unprotected). Cost: stowed for the rest of the day (330 to 350 minutes). Needs a load cell on the winch that the design does not have yet; tension, noise and storms are modelled. In the twin: choose "ATTACK: ..." in Inject a sensor fault, raise the wind above 15 m/s, and switch the monitor on and off.

## Not done / limits
- No hardware: sensors, tension, wind and storms are simulated; the ESP32 sketch has not been flashed.
- Wind loads are screening-level (two coefficient models); a wind-tunnel or CFD study would settle the stow angle.
- The finite element model is 2-D linear elastic with a fixed root; no weld, clamp or fatigue detail.
- The distilled network does not beat the monthly plan in closed loop; the design explorer front is approximate.
- Ideas from a pasted list that were deliberately skipped: reinforcement learning (the exhaustive search is already optimal), physics-informed CFD surrogates, flow-structure coupling, dust CFD and frost (need CFD tools, or do not apply at Madurai), quantum optimisation, satellite mapping and robot vision (off topic).
